## Built

- `policy.rs` (commit `895ea4c0e`): `DEFAULT_FETCH_TIMEOUT_S` (30, now used by `Default`), `MAX_FETCH_TIMEOUT_S` (600) and `FetchPolicy::deadline() -> Option<Duration>` (0 = none, clamped above the max). Serialized shape and fingerprint are unchanged. Tests: 3.
- `fallback_driver.rs` (commit `5427d9e95`):
  - field `deadline: Option<Duration>`, set in `new` and `from_plan` from `default_fetch_deadline()` (the default policy's 30 s when the flag is on, `None` when it is off);
  - builders `with_fetch_policy(&FetchPolicy)` (takes `policy.deadline()` only while the flag is on) and `with_fetch_deadline(Option<Duration>)` (for tests and pinning);
  - in `fetch_inner`: one `tokio::time::Instant` start. Before each registered tier the remaining time is worked out. With none left, the tier is skipped (attempt `skipped: the fetch deadline is spent`) and the chain ends. Otherwise the tier call runs under `tokio::time::timeout(share)`. A stall becomes 504 `TIMEOUT` with `details {reason, driver, slice_ms, deadline_ms}`. TIMEOUT is retryable and transport-class, so the chain moves to the next tier under both `honest` and `legacy`. The message names the tier and the times, never the URL. After the loop, a block or JS-shell response held from an earlier tier is still returned before the deadline error, as before;
  - split: `tier_weight` (browser 2, others 1) and `tier_slice(remaining, weight, weight_left)`. Each tier gets a weighted share of the remaining time, at least `MIN_TIER_SLICE` (250 ms) and never more than what remains. The last tier gets all of it, and time a tier does not use carries over to the tiers after it;
  - `pub mod fetch_deadline_reason { TIER_STALLED = "tier_stalled", DEADLINE_EXCEEDED = "fetch_deadline_exceeded", ALL }`, `FETCH_DEADLINE_ENV`, `fetch_deadline_from_value`, `fetch_deadline_enabled` (OnceLock, one `quarry.fetch_deadline` info line), `default_fetch_deadline`, `stalled_tier_error` and `deadline_exceeded_error`;
  - tests in `fetch_deadline_tests` (12, of which 5 are async with `start_paused = true`): with no deadline a slow tier is waited for, as before; a stalled tier passes the remaining time to the next tier (served at about 16 s of 30); a chain of three stalled tiers returns at 30 s with `fetch_deadline_exceeded` and 3 attempts; once the budget is used up the next tier is skipped and never called; a tier that answers within its share is untouched; plus tests for the share arithmetic, the reason choice, the closed reason list, the flag parser and the 30 s default.

## Flag and default

- `QUARRY_EDGE__FETCH_DEADLINE`, default off. `1`, `true` and `on` (any case, surrounding blanks ignored) turn it on. Read once per process.
- Off: `deadline` is `None`, the tier call is awaited exactly as before, with no new error and no new envelope. `with_fetch_policy` does nothing.
- On: every chain has a 30 s budget, or the caller's `FetchPolicy.timeout_s` when passed through `with_fetch_policy`.
- No compose line and no docs row were added (those files are outside this unit).

## Assumed decisions

1. The sub-codes live in `fallback_driver.rs` (`fetch_deadline_reason`), not in `quarry-core` `error.rs`, which is not in this unit's file list. The code stays 504 TIMEOUT, retryable as TIMEOUT already is: a new call gets a new budget. They are not DRIVER_FAILED sub-codes, so E3.8.1's settle leaves them alone.
2. Two reasons: `tier_stalled` (a tier used up its share while time remained for later tiers) and `fetch_deadline_exceeded` (the share was all the time left, or no time was left for the next tier).
3. Split: each tier gets a weighted share of the remaining time, browser weight 2, others 1. The floor is 250 ms, the last tier gets everything, and only registered tiers count.
4. The default budget when no policy is passed is `FetchPolicy::default()`, 30 s. That is below the 55 s fetch cap and the 60 s and 125 s route deadlines in INNH-1-design.md D14.
5. `timeout_s` above 600 is clamped to `MAX_FETCH_TIMEOUT_S`, and `timeout_s == 0` means no budget.
6. A response held from an earlier tier (a block under legacy, a JS shell) is still returned before the deadline error.
7. On timeout the stalled tier's future is dropped (normal tokio cancellation). A tier that holds a browser lease relies on its own Drop to release it.
