# Acquisition experiments and 90-day execution plan

Internal preparation only · 3 November 2026

All counts, hours and thresholds are proposed operating rules. They are not forecasts, observed conversion rates or industry benchmarks. The plan assumes one founder plus a named delivery owner, with at most two simultaneous pilots.

## Five low-cash experiments, in sequence

| ID / priority | Channel and core message | Why it may work / cost mechanism | Test and owner | Continue / change rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E1 / first | Warm introductions and targeted founder discovery: «Vis oss én servicehenvendelse som krever mye oppfølging.» | Trust reduces explanation and qualification friction; direct observation informs a reusable offer. Founder time is counted. | Founder: interview 15 companies across two adjacent subsegments; review the first ten before selecting the workflow. Proposed cap 30 founder hours plus 5,000 NOK cash. | Continue if at least five independently describe the same repeated task, three provide a measurable sample and two accept a priced pilot proposal. Otherwise revise task/segment before building. |
| E2 / second | Existing ERP/IT advisers: «Én flyt som gjør kundens eksisterende systemer mer nyttige.» | An adviser already has access and trust; repeatable delivery may serve several customers. Recruitment, enablement and revenue share are real costs. | Founder: five adviser conversations after E1; seek two qualified, opt-in introductions from at least one. Proposed cap 12 hours plus 3,000 NOK cash. | Advance when an introduction becomes a paid pilot and the adviser can explain buyer, task and delivery scope. Stop expanding partner recruitment if no qualified introductions result. |
| E3 / after customer proof | Useful operational resource + small industry session: «Hvor blir servicehenvendelsene liggende?» | A task checklist/baseline tool is relevant to a narrow buyer; a customer or willing adviser supplies audience trust. Labour replaces ad spend. | Founder: one ungated checklist, one anonymized demonstration and one small session with an agreed host. Proposed cap 12 hours plus 2,000 NOK cash; no event booked now. | Continue after three qualified buyer conversations and one scoped pilot opportunity within 30 days. Views, downloads and attendance alone do not pass. |
| E4 / after measured value | Customer reference and introduction: «Hvem kjenner du som følger opp samme type saker?» | Peer proof addresses perceived implementation risk. A customer chooses whether and whom to introduce. | Customer owner: ask the first three eligible satisfied pilot/production customers; record accepted intros and resulting qualified companies. Proposed cap 5 hours; incentives require separate approval and cost tracking. | Keep as an acquisition hypothesis until a referred company becomes a paying retained account. Fewer than three eligible customers means the test is incomplete. |
| E5 / product-dependent | Scoped collaboration: «Denne saken trenger din avklaring.» | A colleague or supplier enters to complete useful work. Product value creates relevant exposure. | Product + customer owner: first observe internal colleague review in pilots. Only test external invitations after isolation, permissions and access revocation pass. Initial discovery/prototype cap 8 hours; engineering is separately estimated. | First measure useful review and repeat use. Do not claim a cross-company loop until an invited separate company starts and pays for its own workflow. No external sharing capability is assumed ready. |

Caps are per experiment, exclude pilot delivery/product engineering unless stated, and do not authorize spending. Run E1 first and E2 next; stage the other experiments behind evidence. Do not add all the time budgets to an already full delivery schedule.

### Distribution loop measurements

- Internal adoption: useful colleague reviews / eligible review requests; distinct weekly active workflow users; time to first completed case; retained team use.
- Cross-company exposure: unique invited organizations → accepted invitations → useful actions → separate qualified accounts → paid subscriptions → retained accounts.
- Simple invitation diagnostic: expected new paid accounts per active account = distinct invited organizations per account × acceptance × useful-action rate × independent paid conversion. Count each organization once. Example assumptions 2 × 0.60 × 0.50 × 0.10 = **0.06**, which is supplementary acquisition, not self-sustaining virality.
- Referrals: intros accepted, qualified opportunities, paid accounts, fully loaded referral CAC and 90-day retention. Never add a referral conversion twice to a partner or direct cohort.
- Marketplace discovery: track qualified opportunities and paid accounts, not installed connectors alone. [Tripletex requires integration approval before publication](https://www.tripletex.no/partnerprogram/integrasjoner/); a future listing is a gated channel, not a current asset.

## Days 1–90, starting only after execution authorization

| Period | Work | Evidence to finish | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–14 | Verify 15 target companies and buyer roles; conduct discovery; baseline at least ten task instances in the chosen workflow; examine existing-tool alternative. | Repeated pain, task frequency, source access, buyer, expected value and common stack. | Select one subsegment, one workflow and one systems combination, or continue discovery. |
| Days 15–30 | Price and scope two pilots; verify a real permitted end-to-end path; agree outcome and budget. Keep manual assistance visible. | Written scope, buyer commitment, delivery estimate, product evidence and agreed decision date. | Start no more than two paid pilots if access and product gates pass. Otherwise offer only discovery/prototype work, clearly labelled. |
| Days 31–60 | Complete four-week evaluations; record all reviewer/support/engineering time; test one colleague handoff. Run limited adviser discovery if capacity permits. | Full cohort outcomes including failures; customer-accepted value report; standardized setup procedure. | Continue only the repeatable workflow. Convert a pilot only on explicit buyer acceptance of the production offer. |
| Days 61–90 | Start the next qualified pilot, improve the common package, request permitted references, test one adviser-sourced lead or small session. | Paid use, repeated weeks of adoption, support cost and one plausible second acquisition route. | Decide whether to repeat this segment. Do not hire a sales team or buy broad ads based on signups. |

Proposed learning target: 15 discovery interviews, two completed paid pilots, at least one production conversion and one additional priced opportunity. These targets are not revenue predictions. Delayed access shifts the schedule; do not compress evaluation to preserve a calendar target.

## Proposed gates before increasing spend

1. **Repeatability:** three paying companies use substantially the same workflow; at least 80% of setup steps/configuration reusable; median setup at or below 24 delivery hours after the first pilots. Record the actual distribution, not only the median.
2. **Value:** actual buyer-accepted capacity or financial value supports the price; at least 3× monthly price is a proposed qualification target. Verify realized value separately from theoretical saved minutes.
3. **Economics:** contribution margin at least 70% in the mature workflow and adjusted acquisition payback at most 12 months under conservative observed costs. These are internal targets, not universal SaaS rules. Include model, integration, hosting, support and recurring partner charges.
4. **Retention:** see at least 90 days of paid usage and renewal intent before increasing spend materially; use longer paid renewal cohorts before claiming LTV or product-market fit. A small cohort is weak evidence, even if nobody has churned.
5. **Reliability:** the promised connected workflow and its denied/degraded paths meet the existing [launch requirements](../../launch-promises.md). Product evidence gates a live offer independently of a good interview.

## Weekly review, 30 minutes

Review same-segment conversations, qualified tasks, priced proposals, paid pilots, completed useful cases, paid retention, customer value, failed cases, acquisition hours/cash, delivery hours and support burden. Choose one bottleneck and one experiment for the next week. Assign one owner and a next-action date. Traffic and reach can explain sourcing; they are not the success metric.
