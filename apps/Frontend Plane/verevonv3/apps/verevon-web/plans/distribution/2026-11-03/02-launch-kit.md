# Customer discovery and pilot launch kit

Internal drafts · 3 November 2026 · no messages sent or promises approved

## Qualification card

| Question | Evidence to record |
| --- | --- |
| Does the company fit the chosen segment? | Public industry description and buyer confirmation; employee range is a prospecting assumption. |
| Is the task repeated enough? | Actual weekly/monthly case count, seasonality and coordinator roles. Initially look for 100+ comparable cases/month, subject to measured case value. |
| Is there an owner? | Named operational champion, financial buyer and technical approver, with a reason to act now. |
| Can we measure the current process? | At least ten timed historical/observed cases, including exceptions; avoid cherry-picking easy work. |
| Can we deliver with a shared package? | Same task and common systems as the other pilots; list bespoke work separately. |
| Can a narrow pilot access the necessary evidence? | Authorized sample/export or verified connector; source scope and allowed actions agreed. |
| Is there budget and a decision date? | Buyer accepts a proposed pilot price and scheduled continuation decision before setup. |
| Is the counterfactual credible? | Existing ERP, Microsoft tools, manual process and any other active automation project. |

Decline or re-scope broad transformation projects, compulsory replacement of the ERP, urgent safety-critical diagnosis, autonomous financial decisions, unbounded custom connectors, or buyers who will not measure outcomes. A general-purpose AI evaluation without a workflow owner is not a pilot.

## First 30-minute interview

1. «Kan du vise meg den siste servicehenvendelsen som krevde mye oppfølging?»
2. «Hva gjorde du først, og hvilke systemer måtte du innom?»
3. «Hvor stoppet saken opp? Hvem måtte du spørre?»
4. «Hvor mange lignende saker fikk dere forrige uke?»
5. «Hva koster det når saken blir liggende, eller når informasjonen blir feil?»
6. «Hva har dere allerede prøvd? Hva fungerte, og hva fungerte ikke?»
7. «Hvilke beslutninger kan en koordinator ta selv? Hva må godkjennes?»
8. «Hvem har ansvar for å kjøpe og ta i bruk noe som hjelper med dette?»
9. «Kunne vi målt dette på et lite, avgrenset utvalg? Hva måtte vært på plass først?»
10. Only after the process is understood: show the proposed result and test whether the buyer would pay for a scoped pilot.

Record actual words, observed steps and unknowns. The Norwegian copy below is proposed messaging, not customer testimony.

## Proposed offer: serviceforespørsel → klargjort sak

**Scope:** one team, one case type, 3–8 named users, two or three selected evidence sources, four weeks and a fixed case-volume cap agreed before purchase. Aim for at least 30 comparable evaluated cases; extend the evaluation or report low confidence if volume is insufficient.

**Commercial hypotheses, NOK excluding VAT:** pilot 15,000; ongoing package test at 7,500/month with an initial usage limit; standard production setup 10,000. These are discovery anchors, not a published price list. The pilot does not automatically include production rollout. At most 5,000 of the pilot may be credited against production setup if explicitly agreed and economically affordable. Record cash, credits, implementation time and failed pilots accurately.

**Proposed sequence:**

| Period | Customer work | Verevon work | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before start | Buyer, champion and technical owner agree task and baseline | Verify allowed product behaviour; cost the scope | Signed scope and continuation decision date |
| Week 1 | Supply an authorized sample and explain exceptions | Establish baseline and configure one repeatable flow | Before/after measurement sheet and reviewed test cases |
| Week 2 | Evaluate prepared cases in parallel with current process | Measure quality and reviewer time; correct failure modes | First measured result and exception list |
| Week 3 | Repeat with a second authorized colleague | Assess useful sharing and delivery burden | Repeatability and team-use evidence |
| Week 4 | Decide continue, change or stop | Present complete results and costs | Buyer decision and scoped rollout proposal |

**Success criteria to negotiate before purchase:**

- Useful prepared output on at least 80% of eligible evaluated cases; report ineligible/missing-source cases separately and retain them in total workload economics.
- At least 30% lower median hands-on handling time for comparable cases, including review and correction. Also report total team hours and outliers.
- At least two authorized users completing the workflow in each of the final two weeks.
- No unauthorized external or financial action; wrong critical facts are failures, not acceptable drafts.
- Credible monthly capacity value at least 3× the proposed monthly price, using the customer's assumptions and a conservative realization factor.
- A buyer willing to pay for continued use; a positive interview or free pilot is not payment evidence.

These are proposed decision rules, not predictions or claims about existing performance. Small pilot samples do not prove long-term retention or statistical certainty.

**Stop conditions:** insufficient source access, unsafe/misleading outputs, a materially different task, or setup exceeding the agreed cap. Pause the affected flow, document the issue, then offer a narrowed scope or stop decision. Do not silently add unpaid bespoke work.

## Adviser conversation

**Target:** an ERP, Microsoft 365 or IT-service adviser already serving several businesses in the chosen segment. Examples can be researched through [Visma's public directory](https://www.vismasoftware.no/finn-din-partner); none is currently a confirmed Verevon partner.

**Proposed message:** «Vi vil teste én avgrenset serviceflyt hos bedrifter dere allerede hjelper. Dere beholder kundeansvaret; vi tar produkt- og pilotleveransen. Før vi diskuterer et partnerprogram, ønsker vi å forstå om to av kundene deres har samme problem.»

Progression: introduction → jointly qualified pilot → delivery reviewed → reusable onboarding guide → one trained implementation partner. Discuss a capped one-time referral fee **or** a recurring revenue share after successful collection, not both by default. Set scope, attribution, customer ownership, support responsibilities and termination before signing anything.

## Customer relationship routine

- Named commercial and delivery owners; agree actual service hours before promising an SLA.
- Weekly pilot review: completed cases, exceptions, saved hands-on time, spend and next change.
- Monthly paid-customer review: realized value, cost, unresolved problems, authorized next workflow and export needs.
- Ask for a reference or introduction after evidence, and separately obtain permission for publication.
- A failed pilot receives an honest outcome report. Record why it failed so the next prospect is better qualified.

## CRM fields and stage exits

Use the existing CRM if available; no new vendor or integration is needed to begin. No prospects or personal details have been entered in this package.

Fields: account ID, company, segment, public source URL, acquisition source, introduction permission, champion role, buyer role, task, monthly volume, baseline minutes, systems, allowed pilot data, access blocker, price tested, stage, stage-entry date, next action/date/owner, sales hours, engineering hours, cash spend, pilot fee/cost/credit, outcome, lost reason, paid-start date, renewal date, realized monthly value and support hours.

Stages: researched → relevant conversation booked → task qualified → pilot scoped → paid pilot → active evaluation → production subscription → retained at 90 days. Each advance requires corresponding evidence; retain closed-lost and failed-pilot records in cost calculations. Separate partner-sourced, customer-referred and direct accounts, and deduplicate by company.
