# Thirty starting places: customer niches paired with distribution routes

Prepared 3 November 2026. Internal research and recommendations only. No prospect has been contacted, scored as a confirmed buyer, enrolled or added to an external CRM. No publication, deployment or automation has been activated.

## How to read the rankings

The founder requested a mix of **customer types and places to reach them**. Each row therefore pairs a niche with a concrete acquisition route, a task to test, agent assistance and human work.

- **Best:** ten recommended combinations for early fit, repeatability, affordable learning and access to a buyer. These are judgments, not measured conversion rankings.
- **Easiest:** ten combinations with a low barrier to an initial research, conversation or draft experiment. Easy to start does not mean easy to close or low fully loaded CAC.
- **Hardest:** ten combinations with greater product, access, procurement or repeatability barriers. Keep these as later options, not ten more channels to launch.
- **A — Agent-assisted:** source research, qualification, comparison, drafting or analysis can form a substantial repeatable workflow. A person still owns communication, discovery and commercial decisions.
- **H — Human-led:** relationships, live operational understanding, permission, negotiation or procurement dominate. Agents can support preparation but do not remove the main work.

**Sorting:** A rows come first as requested; rows are ranked within that group. H rows follow, ranked within their group. Row numbers are the resulting display order, not a claim that an automated route is commercially better than a warm introduction. In the hardest list, harder combinations come first within each group. No automation percentage or sales-cycle estimate is presented as a fact.

**Verevon's role:** all uses in the agent column are proposed ways to use Verevon internally and demonstrate it to buyers. The task did not verify live source, CRM, email, marketplace or calendar connections. Until verified, run the brief on an authorized source pack and review the output manually. See [agent workflow briefs](08-verevon-distribution-agents.md).

Directory memberships, fees, event availability, publication rules and permission to reuse data must be checked for the particular activity before execution. Public membership is evidence of affiliation, not purchase intent or permission for a campaign.

These are generally unattractive first acquisition bets for the present plan. Their research preparation may be automatable while winning and implementing them remains difficult. Difficulty is our assessment of likely barriers, not verified buyer timelines.

## What I would actually start with

Do not run thirty campaigns. Keep the previous plan's first discovery sample of **ten service/maintenance firms plus five adjacent industrial suppliers**:

1. Five independent pump/hydraulic/mechanical-service candidates, found through public company information and any genuine warm access.
2. Five B2B ventilation/cooling service candidates from VKE, qualified for actual service coordination.
3. Five industrial equipment suppliers with after-sales work; test whether a willing ERP/IT adviser can introduce one or two.

These are proposed sample counts, not collected prospects. If a real relationship opens another repeatable industrial-service niche, let evidence outrank the desk-research list.

In parallel, research three potentially suitable advisers through the Visma directory. Confirm their industrial customer focus before asking for any introduction. Keep the first offer as **service enquiry → sourced, prepared job → human review**, and compare it with what the customer's current ERP/service tools can already do.
